Read by Anonymous

*Published in 100 subtexts, possibly 2026, possibly as far back as 2018

Cupid’s strongest bow*

[over a blankness] is shot:

strung taut on the back of tensions before,

just how the German newspapers show no more murders

*with trinkets and chains of love-objects.

We know

carving names on trees

is the best way to reexamine yourself,

were you not within the proscenium arch of your eyelids.

You ask:

Can you hear the hum of some far-off song?

How can you not hear that music?

How can you not hear that voice?

How can you not hear that --

(Oh, won’t you release me?)

[With gazes entangled,

be gross with each other, young people,

but only in a purely summative sense, like

you’re tripping over toes].

In the type -

set (of graphic art):

the three-legged stride of such stone statues

has crossed this landscape many times before.

And to crown the suicide of philosophy

with a plastic rim of thorns,

we gather round a lake of piss --

knowing Echo and Narcissus held the same convers(at)ion before,

in an imaginary year B.C.

Now, now -- conflict is the combustion engine

of a narrative train thrown off its tracks,

b u t y o u.

You are the Other to the equation

as sprites and faeries enjoy a hedonism beyond the body of man,

whose poems are worn out like old denim, like these

(what can only spelt in)

Words, words, words.

When all of your advice

aims to make me worse,

[and your feet don’t go anywhere

but only step on things

trying to prove that truth

Can ‘only’ ever be drastic –]

Won’t you release me, please?

Won’t you let me know you’re there at least?

Michael Thomas SmithWriter, scholar, and educator
