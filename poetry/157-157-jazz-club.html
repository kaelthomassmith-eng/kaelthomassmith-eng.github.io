Poem 157

Jazz Club

*Published in West Trade Review in 2018
*Republished in Corncrake in 2026
Journal / archive ↗
Jazz is
when you write a sentence with
only punctuation. A stop. Which only had
prevented. Another stop, from coming.
The bass player, moved His. Mouth for words that
Were not only there, but
Weren’t anywhere.
The heat could have provided a speech bubble
the sweat came out of our bodies like
a whisper.
But no, one --
 
I opened my mouth
to see what (would fly in)
Let’s say goodnight to the moon --
These lines didn’t ask you to listen
(they only asked that you pretend you do). It’s like a relationship
between a person with himself
 
Cain killed Abel but maybe
He thought he was doing him a favor.
I spent most of the time not listening to what you said,
but what you didn’t.
 
(Like the -- )
 
And the drums and drinks and drivel provided a reason
For
Us. To be in a spell – ing de-li-rum.
The notes were practically racist --
one trying to run away from another. Even though
they were only on a staff.
 
It’s a love note by someone who is too young
in their years.
It’s a love scrawled by a drunkard too full
To spell correctly --
 
To express their love, and so there’s but looks away.
Like love is the sun, which it is -- but also
Which it cannot be.
