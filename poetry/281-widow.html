Widow

Published in Plath Profiles in 2017
She dropped everything to become a wife, And to take that honorable title. To scrawl “Mrs.” unto a foreign life, Now her own, now her letters hyetal. Dropping the world else for commitment aught, She’d picture themselves one day cotching old In winter; and starve the present to naught But stories to look back on and be told. Her hand entangled in her husband’s ret, She thought it selfless to suffer abuse -- Her hand always out of her own pocket. She thought it moral to excuse her use. And then he died, and she was not a wife. She was just a word colored in by strife.
