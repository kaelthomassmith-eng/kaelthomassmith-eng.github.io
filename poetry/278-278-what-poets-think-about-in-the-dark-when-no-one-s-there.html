Poem 278

What Poets Think About in the Dark When No One’s There…

*Published in Chewers by Masticadores in 2026
Published version ↗   Journal / archive ↗
Poets love to write about shit they know nothing about.
But they hide that fact behind a sloppy line break.
They stack words together like refugees
canned into a space too small for them.
 
That’s probably too much, which – excess --
is something poets claim they don’t do,
when all poetry depends on excess, i.e.
the strange proliferation of lecherous words.
 
I’d allude to Mallarme if I believed you read him
(or at least stumbled through his verbal marsh).
Oh, to make you fall into a tiny death,
to tickle your fancy until you laugh your life away.
 
This is a poem I wore for myself -- full coverage,
but I expect you to be a voyeur and look through it.
Meanwhile, I’d carve your name on a bar stool
like fools do on the naked torsos of trees.
 
Only this etch marks who you feel
envy for the most, for this round’s never
on me, because it’s ultimately the fattest lips
that say the darned kindest things.
 
Sometimes we find our philosophy
written on the bottom of a bottle,
after the world has slipped
into a soft oblivion. Excess:
 
How I’d dance on this floor,
to some music that is never played,
when the dark has come to tuck in
the children of this plight.
 
I smoked my fingers with a lit cigarette
into the ember of a religion of old -- brutalized
by the very evils of the new boss. Can’t you see
how all things are the same? -- I don’t even try.
