Old Man at the Café

Or

Coffee

(a cover of Jacques Prévert)

*Published in HCE Review in 2018.
*Republished in Yuang Yang somewhere around 2020

Within a world of winding sidewalks,

I saw an old man outside a French café --

here, the same arrogant sun

(so unoriginal, a mere copy of all other days)

did duel with his pocked-face

but couldn’t do anything to his expression.

By myself, I would have solemnly guessed

Prospeta walking by with a basket of fruit

dumped her rotting spoils around the place

before she ran back to April on spring-heels --

just as soon as she ran from it the year before.

The old man hunched over his lonely table

with the weight of time bending him down,

like a tired Macbeth reading his last line.

And on his small lap had jumped

a maverick drop of brown coffee lost.

The morn had turned sunny side down,

his entire presence -- unassuming,

shadowing his frail hand

as it wiped his corduroy pants.

I knew he lived his life in the brackets;

I knew he tried a weak divination,

staring into the grounds of his cup --

his whole life was one endless cup,

for the lines of his face told a greater story

than the lines written down here.

And the man could barely lift his hand

to salute the joe, whose steam

rose quicker than the hand.

Its entrails seemed to grab him

just as hard as he grabbed the past.

And in the orb of my eye

it seemed the joys of the world had left him --

(no more to jump, no more to run

no more to – I can’t).

Mothers had left the picture for even longer.

Even his scone seemed to not meet his mouth,

let alone my thoughts,

which are held captive by his hat.

I know my prejudice clouds the mind

(am I even able to understand the daily?),

for he seemed fine, but I ran outside crying.

Michael Thomas SmithWriter, scholar, and educator
