Metroid; Or, Everything I Needed to Know About Feminism

*Published in Call me [Brackets] in 2020

Is it feminism if it wears a mask;

Or,

must feminism always wear a mask –

like a philosophy that asks questions,

Socratic

but alone in the dead

Of space?

Are masks a needed accessory

for all (space)women --

like one necessary for breathing itself?

Is it feminism if you curl up

into a ball --

even if shooting targets

even if dropping bombs?

From roping oneself along

glass ceilings,

higher than the planet itself

to

harness the Metroid –

not a brain but only a simulacrum,

only the image of them, more true–

a parasite,

which seems like the perfect analogy for canon.

And in this game --

if you call a curse that --

only later can it be a

first

person view.

“I’m a different person now,”

she said through grins and moans

like a sword swallower who, for meals,

consumes the metallic manner of your robot tone.

“Led me alone,”

for the space we least explore

is the metaphorical.

Is it feminism if it is a question --

like who’s Samus:

Noun, of an origin long,

who could or couldn’t be?

Michael Thomas SmithWriter, scholar, and educator
