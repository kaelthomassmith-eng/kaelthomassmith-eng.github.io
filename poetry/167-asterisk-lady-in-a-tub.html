Asterisk (Lady in a Tub)

Published in Pennsylvania Literary Journal in 2018
She stared at herself in the uncooperative mirror in front of the Two Great Black Fellows as would frighten a lady out of herself; she stared into asterisks from which to behold. She broke the bathwater with the tip of her toe and wouldn't look into its Charybris waves, longing for the days yet-to-have-been when we can narrate our own crystal balls. Because not even the filthy ring around this tub can hold our whole grimy world together (we’re drowning in a foot of water, she thought, and we haven’t even gotten into it yet). She soaked with a face as long as a yard in the clock's slouched hours until the water was not warm as when she ducked in. Then, her heart galloped into a round-a-bend. ‘Twas not the chill to give her neuralgia: (she had attacked him with a fake smile, and history will say he lost the war but ultimately won the battle). She stood up and the water ran away down her skin like a thousand refugees. And she quoted, “If you desired it. For you, I would be nothing, or merely a wet footprint.” But the papered room did not respond out of sheer impoliteness. But even he was not invited into his own thoughts (according to the dictionary anyway). If no one can see the magnificence of a queen sitting naked at an empty table, then who can dine on her mignonette cloth? Who, then, is able?
