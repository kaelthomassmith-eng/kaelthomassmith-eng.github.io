Love letters from a spaceman to the goddess of the moon:

Or, the last man on earth

*Published in Dryland in 2013

Dear Cynthia,

Walking backwards in my Orlan skin

But what thick space suit closes me in?

Haloes of meteors circle my head

Ten thousand miles apart each –

But the space between my thoughts is not quite as dead

Dear Cynthia,

Did you know of the myth of the man on the moon?

Of the earthy man who grabbed it by his teeth too soon…

Of the waves of heat that won’t dissipate

-particles too small to touch me –

But feel I do, the wrath of fate

Dear Cynthia,

And counting blips from off my thumb

Is all a matter of relative sums.

A voice in a void can never resist

- My helmetless head knows this –

Yet a hand on my lips cannot cease to resist

Dear Cynthia

Did you know the cold of space to be so grim?

Is the music of the spheres writ on a whim?

Or improvised by godly hues?

All I do is turn knobs and dials

To relegate these nebulous blues

Dear Cynthia,

A coursing down upon your grey pocked skin

If I sing, “I” numbers a fateful hymn

Your orlanze style stole my breath

From my stretched image on your face

Like Charon profiting from bitter death

Dear Cynthia,

And all the words I have to say to you

Cannot be crammed in dirty haiku

There is no dark side of this rock I’m on –

Likened to caged fun

But can you recycle light from the sun?

Dear Cynthia,

Venus is the goddess of the love letter

For clouds of Sulfuric Acid can reflect a smile better

Yet are you the love that always watched?

To stretch me thin across miles of space,

And measure my height with a bedpost notched

Dear Cynthia,

Who can count to infinity first?

Plucking petals off of a supernova’s burst

Atoms dance like a roll of the die

Hold the earth between forefinger and thumb

A quantum entanglement you and I.

Michael Thomas SmithWriter, scholar, and educator
