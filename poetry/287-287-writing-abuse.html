Michael Thomas Smith
Poetry
Poem 287

Writing Abuse

*Published in Pilcrow and Dagger in 2018
Beat me to the floor again,
for I spend more time licking the linoleum,
than mopping up my sloppy words.
It was a long time coming
but a longer time gone --
because even dumb words cry loudly
when uttered by the heart.
 
We don’t talk about things;
we only put them down into paragraphs.
 
Holding opinions
Min turns the word over on the page,
with a snap --
and I’m put back into a shack,
a hold not big enough to contain these emotions.
Our highs move laws meet to meet,
so it happens again…
 
“My love” – the word has abused me again.
Yet again.
Why do I write? What delirium to me unknown
dips this venereal quill --
and then brings the tip to hone?
 
I don’t know.
This desk’s like a roundtable for two,
and in this pas de deux
I wanted to sit as far away from myself
as possible,
but there wasn’t a chair,
so in my idleness
I become a recidivist of masochism.
 
The ink sinks its teeth into my skin,
and its poison spreads again –
I go back to the trace of words
that call me ‘fool’ and me do gird.
 
There’s tremors in my hand,
and similar likeness in my mind.
 
And in this nisus
of slow death passing,
this surly pen
talks back to me more than I can talk for myself.
 
A dilettanti
of self-abuse,
I am such as the pen is to paper,
abnegating its connection to the mind.
 
I cannot escape my abusive lover:
Me, drooling with a baby’s spittle,
trying to hobble the long road away
to a heart fickle
(and talking to an audience).
It’s like forming a love triangle with the therapist.
