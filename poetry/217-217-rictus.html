Poem 217

Rictus

*Published in Harbinger in 2018
Journal / archive ↗
Did you hear that mumpsimus,
Saying those starved words over
the mound? Excuse me: mumpsimus, n.:
“a person who obstinately adheres to old ways
in spite
of clear evidence that they are wrong.”
This is the OED, who should aid all people
in their revenge – but
onto those other words;
those spoken by the above,
said through a mayonnaise tooth:
“the dead will live on
in the memories of those who survive them.”
Really?
This is why the dead laugh still,
at a statement to be buried:
To live a few more years,
by solace brought in a pocket
soon to join the dearly parted,
who’ll be reunited with a taut smile
in this ceremony of Dalian ants.
