You were but I was (A sonnet inspired poem)

Published in North of Oxford, 15 November 2018; Republished in Feed the Holy, 15 October 2026
The day was exhausted, as much were we. When you said those words, you said them with teeth. looking within me, eyes minatory. I peered instead at your tone underneath. Were I to charge, a fee placed on ego, my throat, a prisoner, wouldn’t react: You aimed directly at my morillo to stop those quite words right in their tracks. The War of the Roses entered act two: to make me cow to your vile rhetoric, all those castrating winds, to me, you blew What am I? Selfish, immature, a dick? Beside the lonely road, where no movement Is allowed, ‘cept my heart invisible Our car pavisanding, its wax lucent, With a wheel stuck for greater principles You said you were kidding – spoke in a buzz, that I wasn’t that. You were but I was.
