Zelda; Or Everything I Needed to Learn About Economics

Published in Call Me [Brackets], Issue 4, Spring 2020; Republished in Feed the Holy, 18 November 2026
A plenum of air is all that you own -- until you’re smacking through your neighbor’s flower pots, collecting rupees for some quest or not. The open land of Hyrule, a monarchic kingdom, is a reliquary of delirium that requires you to collect all you can, For holding onto things indefinitely is the nature of economy, and we could all buy any single item, given the understanding we never need eat, rest, or sleep. Green is the color of tave, Of money, heroes, him -- In a fantasy world where “and to save” Is a bitter contronym. So you trim the grass for a rupee, carrying more items than possible -- and then once you have those, it’s easier to get more, Until you own clothes for the part when no one else owns a second pair, and it requires some evil imbroglio For you to be the chosen one here Because you’re always one more link in a chain of a long economic fetter, in which history repeats itself painfully with the same evil rising anon, Until someone writes the happy ending -- The people, they may fall, but the kingdom never did. You’re always the hero as a kid.
