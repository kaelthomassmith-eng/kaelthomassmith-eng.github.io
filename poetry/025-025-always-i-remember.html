Michael Thomas Smith
Poetry
Poem 25

Always, I Remember

*Published in Valley Voices in 2020.
Always, I remember --
I, who am not familiar with myself.
These imprints of a past
that shall not be,
a draft of delirium
imported into my head
 
Directly like the memory
of a movie once seen
in childhood,
but since lost to responsibility.
 
These things like clouds
but heavy as concrete
shall not let me be,
of which I shall never be free.
 
Like a dog tied to a string,
my mind is wilderness undoing,
circling its stake in festejo,
but with no partner to grasp.
 
It solemnly drifts through
the marsh of the trace,
a jobbernowl that is
lost in some other day
 
and as soon as I try and grasp it,
I transform, much like a
precedent of someone else’s
first edition ethics.
 
As our words Antony Over
because no one wants them
when they speak a truth,
these zeligs of mental health.
 
For memories are tattoos
nunc stans --
they remain a companion
who make you alone.
 
And as the bodies of the days
pile up, when one day leans
on another until they both
collapse into the year’s end.
 
Always, I remember --
Despite all my efforts to forget.
