Michael Thomas Smith
Poetry
Poem 54

Communication / Stand Apart

*Published in Valley Voices in 2020
Words tripping over each other,
Colliding in a street
So high, they have altitude sickness -
My tongue a Gordian knot:
 
Oh, for some preceptor
To dictate my feelings,
Instead of being left to beg
For a dime on a cold street.
 
We might call this “wisdom:”
When the Papamama
Repeats itself in whispers
On the wind for generations…
 
I have the loftiest ideas
When I’m feeling down and out --
And wonder why I speak from mountaintops
When we’re standing face to face,
 
But separated by a wall of culture,
Like a gilded ruin that stands everywhere
Yet eaten by vegetation,
Such that sustenance is the consumer.
 
Who knew that food itself
Could have an eating addiction?
Consumption must eat itself,
And I hope these words a feast.
 
But yet I speak - the weight of sound
Dragging its measured feet,
Trying to get to you
In an alleyway of voices.
 
But yet I’ll speak –
Sacrificing most of my lies,
Until something I say
dings in your head. I’ll try.
