Epiphany

*Published in HEART in 2018.

Writing poetry is basically like getting other people to say,

“Hey, I like your brain.”

Writing poetry is like an abusive relationship (this one goes out to all the writers out there -- not necessarily on the dance floor -- because you probably got a note that read ‘we can’t find a place for this right now,’ which is a phrase that makes it hard to find a place to dance).

Writing poetry is being weepy…but totally trying to make yourself not sound weepy, like do you even “ask ye why these sad tears stream?”

Writing poetry is like writing the above line, but making a note to myself during a break from the writing of the above line: ‘make cloying poetic line.’ (It’s also wishing that there was some parenthetical comment there).

Writing poetry is a good way to make your parents uneasy, which also – admittedly – is also a good way to have fun.

Writing poetry is tying your brain to something and questioning the very nature of “the will:” what you will and whether you will it or not (particularly in the most Shakespearean way possible).

Writing poetry is having your tongue do Olympian level jumping jacks, when all the while your brain does some opposing routine in the opposite direction.

Writing poetry is doing drugs not to be creative but to see how creative you were.

Writing poetry is not saying the above line (because …because you can fill in one hundred things after the word ‘because’).

Writing poetry is making the most of an addiction, which in turn is making the most of ‘the surplus,’ abstracted.

Writing poetry is to be a capitalist in the most creative sense of the term. It’s Keynesian in its crosses and (if possible) double crosses. It can conceive and re-conceive money, value, wealth, savings, and -- it ever will cut this sentence here…but it will still build a legacy upon the power of ‘re-‘

Writing poetry is standing on the power of a comma. It’s knowing that this punctuation can shake the world like Mount Vesuvius at her most irate – that ‘irate-ness,’ like most emotions, is just a matter of punctuation.

Writing poetry is like breathing – but not because you have to, but because you want to –

Or think you want to

Or think you want to want to…

Writing poetry is viewing the ‘name’ and the ‘thing’ like the chicken and the egg, but also understanding how that vision presupposes the very thing it claims to do and in the doing analyze, while also inverting that phrase to ‘…presupposes analyzing the doing of the thing in the doing’ (which in itself presupposes), while also understanding both of those sentiments wholly even though no one can even definitively claim they are understandable…unless we’re talking purely about definitions. Or things.

Writing poetry is letting a sentence run-on purely for emotional purposes.

Writing poetry is drawing a family tree only without any family members on it. It’s appreciating the branches purely for themselves.

Writing poetry is an obscenity.

Michael Thomas SmithWriter, scholar, and educator
